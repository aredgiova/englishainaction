## Mission 1 content

Source: S1, sections 1–9. Designed for autonomous, self-paced learning across Grades 1–11. Approximate duration 2–3 hours; the detailed suggested schedule totals 155 minutes.

Objectives: recognize and use greetings and farewells; introduce oneself and others; ask and answer names and wellbeing; use subject pronouns and basic affirmative, negative and interrogative forms of *to be*; use classroom/social expressions; understand short introductions; produce a short oral and written introduction.

Core vocabulary: Hello, Hi, Good morning, Good afternoon, Good evening, Goodbye, Bye, See you, Thank you, Please, Sorry, Excuse me, Nice to meet you, Welcome.

Personal information: Name, First name, Last name, Age, Country, City, Student, Teacher, Friend.

Core expressions: What’s your name? My name is… I’m… How are you? I’m fine, thank you. Nice to meet you. This is… Who is he? Who is she? He is… She is…

Grammar mappings: I/am; you/are; he/is; she/is; it/is; we/are; they/are. Contractions: I’m, you’re, he’s, she’s, it’s, we’re, they’re.

## Mission 2 content

Source: S2, Part II, Mission 2. Story: “The student enters Explorer Village. The village is full of objects, places and people, but its information screens have lost their labels. Mia needs help identifying the items and organizing the explorer's surroundings.”

Objectives:

- Identify familiar objects and people.
- Ask and answer simple questions about objects.
- Use basic descriptive language.
- Use articles and demonstratives in familiar contexts.
- Understand and produce simple sentences about the immediate environment.

Initial language scope: everyday objects and familiar places; basic descriptive adjectives; classroom and personal items; this, that, these, those; a/an; basic singular/plural forms; “What's this?” and “What are these?”; pronunciation of object names and plural endings where relevant. The source says the final scope should follow the approved Unit 2 syllabus, which was not supplied.

## Gaps and source inconsistencies

1. Mission 2 lacks an approved vocabulary list, exact scripts, item-level questions/answers, short readings, scoring, detailed screens and duration. Any concrete prototype examples added for it are authored draft content, not quoted syllabus.
2. Mission 1 mentions affirmative, negative and interrogative *to be* outcomes but the exact practice predominantly trains affirmative forms. A complete implementation should add or identify explicit negative/question practice and flag that extension as authored material.
3. The Mission 1 final reading profile and its three questions are unspecified. Reusing a practice reading would reduce novelty; an original equivalent profile is a reasonable clearly documented prototype addition.
4. S1 screen 14 is titled “The First Crystal” and displays a first crystal after Mission 1. S2 says all eight mission keys activate the Crystal of Identity at Mission 8. The coherent early module reward is one map key plus a badge, reserving the world crystal for Mission 8.
5. S4's early story concerns map fragments and a “World of Communication”; S2's fuller narrative uses the Global Language Network and eight crystals. Use the fuller Story Bible for narrative continuity, retaining map keys as mission rewards.
6. Four named main characters are specified in DOCX; the ZIP establishes a female Professor Nova. The initially attached collage is a broad visual reference and shows a different generic scientist. Use the named ZIP references for character consistency.
7. No production audio, animation, clean illustration, recordings or assessment service is supplied. Browser speech synthesis can support a prototype but should not be represented as finalized recorded audio or validated pronunciation assessment.
8. The same A1.1 objectives cover Grades 1–11 with different presentation needs. A single colorful initial visual treatment is a prototype for one audience experience, not proof of implemented adaptations for every grade.
9. Source records include completed, needs practice and mastered as separate statuses. XP, participation and badges must not be presented as academic mastery. A local browser prototype can save its own practice progress but cannot claim institutional reporting or Moodle integration without actual integration.

## Shared design rules drawn from the sources

Use brief supportive feedback and unlimited retries; no point deductions or permanent failures. Preserve replay and review access. Provide transcripts and keyboard navigation. Age and country are optional, fictional identities are acceptable, and voice recording is optional. Store only data justified by the selected implementation; the source's architecture is a proposal, not a request to send learner data externally.
